ABOUT MERIDIAN

About Meridian

Meridian Dispute Resolution is a Saskatchewan-based mediation practice offering a structured, confidential process for resolving disputes outside of court — available virtually or in person, across Canada.

About Mabel Adekola

I spent fifteen years in technical support, working on problems requiring structured diagnosis, clear communication under pressure, and translation between competing and often conflicting perspectives.

Over time, the work shifted away from technical issues and toward resolving misunderstandings between people operating from different assumptions. That experience informs my approach to dispute resolution: clarity, structure, and disciplined communication.

I have consistently been the person others turn to when a disagreement needs to be worked through, in the family, at the workplace or in the community. I have been described as blunt, logical, and unsentimental. In this context, those are assets.

I founded Meridian to formalize this within a defined mediation practice, with clear process boundaries, a neutral role, and no incentive to prolong disputes beyond what is necessary.

I am a member of the ADR Institute of Saskatchewan (ADRSK), a regional affiliate of the ADR Institute of Canada (ADRIC). I have completed ADRIC's introductory mediation training and am currently completing the additional specialized training required for the Qualified Mediator (Q.Med) designation.

What to Expect

I do not take sides. My role is to keep the discussion productive where direct communication has broken down.

Before any process begins, I assess whether mediation is appropriate for the situation. If it is not, I will state that at the initial consultation.

If mediation proceeds, it follows a structured and confidential process designed to support negotiation between participants. The objective is not agreement at any cost — it is clarity and resolution where feasible.

Limitations of Service

Meridian Dispute Resolution does not provide legal, financial, or tax advice, or psychological counselling.

It does not act as an advocate for either party.

It is mediation, a distinct process with a defined structure and purpose.
