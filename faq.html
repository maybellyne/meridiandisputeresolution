FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Answers before you reach out.

These are the questions people most often ask before scheduling a consultation. If you don't see what you're looking for, feel free to reach out directly.

What is mediation, and how is it different from going to court?

Mediation is a structured conversation between the parties in a dispute, facilitated by a neutral third party, called a mediator, who has no stake in the outcome. Unlike court, no judge or arbitrator decides the result. The parties work toward an agreement they can both accept, and the mediator's role is to keep that conversation productive, not to rule on who is right.

Is mediation legally binding?

Mediation itself is not legally binding. If the parties reach an agreement, Meridian documents it in a written memorandum of understanding (MOU). This document is not drafted or reviewed as a legal instrument, so parties are encouraged to have independent legal counsel review it before signing.

Do I need a lawyer?

No, a lawyer is not required to participate in mediation. That said, independent legal advice is recommended before signing any resolution agreement, so you understand your rights and how the terms affect you.

What types of disputes does Meridian handle?

Meridian mediates family and separation matters, estate and elder disputes, workplace conflict, business and commercial disagreements, and neighbour or community disputes. Details on each area are on the How We Help page.

Can I refuse to participate, or what if the other party won't participate?

Mediation is voluntary for everyone involved. Either party can decline to participate or can withdraw at any point. If the other party hasn't yet agreed to mediate, Meridian offers an add-on service where we reach out to them on your behalf to explain how the process works.

Does mediation satisfy Saskatchewan's mandatory Early Family Dispute Resolution requirement?

Saskatchewan requires family law matters going to court to first attempt an approved dispute resolution process. Our mediators are not yet on the Minister's List, the province's list of recognized providers for this requirement. If your matter is subject to this requirement, confirm with Meridian whether mediation here will satisfy it before proceeding.

How does virtual mediation work?

If you prefer virtual mediation, sessions are conducted by videoconferencing. You will need a private space, a stable internet connection, and a device with a camera and microphone. Virtual mediation follows the same structure as an in-person session, and works well for parties in different locations or with scheduling constraints.

Why does Meridian communicate only by email and text?

Meridian does not conduct business over the phone. Email is preferred since it creates a clear, written record of scheduling, instructions, and agreements, and allows both parties to respond on their own time. Text is also available as a convenient alternative.

How should I prepare for a mediation session?

Come with a clear sense of what matters to you and what outcome you'd consider acceptable. Gather any documents relevant to the dispute beforehand. Meridian will send a preparation outline ahead of the first session with more specific guidance for your situation.

What happens during the free consultation?

The consultation is a 30-minute conversation to discuss your situation, explain how mediation works, and determine whether it's a good fit. There's no obligation to proceed afterward, and no fee for the session.

How much does mediation cost, and who pays?

Mediation is $150 CAD per hour, plus applicable taxes. The party who initiates the mediation is responsible for payment, due within 24 hours of each session by e-transfer or cheque.

What if I need to cancel or reschedule?

Each matter includes one complimentary reschedule. Beyond that, cancellations require at least 24 hours' notice. Late cancellations and no-shows are billed at the full session rate.

How long does the process take?

It depends on the complexity of the dispute and how many sessions are needed. Some matters resolve in a single session, while others take several. There's no fixed timeline, since the pace is set by what the parties need to reach a workable outcome.

What happens if we don't reach an agreement?

If no agreement is reached, the mediation simply ends without one. Nothing discussed during mediation binds either party, and all legal rights and options, including litigation, remain fully available afterward.
